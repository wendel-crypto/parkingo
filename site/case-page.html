Parkin’go

A parking app for Miami, dressed in neon. Find a garage from Brickell to Lincoln Road, reserve a bay, follow the route, check in with a QR pass, add time and keep every receipt. Taken from Figma wireframes to a working prototype.

The problem

Finding a spot should not take longer than the drive.

In Miami you circle Brickell looking for a bay, pay at a meter that wants its own app, and watch the clock because the sign says two hours. Garages have room, but the driver cannot see it, cannot hold a bay and cannot add time without walking back.

Parkin’go puts the whole stop in one flow: choose a garage, reserve a numbered bay, drive there with a route, show a QR pass at the gate, extend from your phone and check out with a receipt.

Every screen asks for one decision, with large targets, for a driver who has one eye on the road.

Process

From a Toronto wireframe to a Miami product.

The concept started in Figma as a low-fidelity flow with sample garages near downtown Toronto, priced in Canadian dollars. The wireframes fixed the order of decisions before any color existed: set up a vehicle and a card, find a spot, get there, check in, manage the stay.

The high-fidelity pass added a calm teal system on white cards. This build keeps the flow and moves it to Miami: US dollars, a Brickell, Wynwood and Miami Beach map, five invented garages, favorites, a running session timer, receipts and statistics, in English, Spanish and Portuguese from one file.

Design decisions

Six choices that keep a short stop short.

01

A price on every pin

The map shows the hourly rate on each pin, so choosing between a garage and a beach lot happens before you open a single screen.

02

One bay, one number

The reserved bay, A12, follows you from the bay picker to the route, the arrival screen and the QR pass, so you never have to look it up again.

03

Time you can see

After check-in the stay becomes a countdown that lives on Explore and Activity. Extending is two taps away.

04

Filters for real needs

Covered, EV charging and Favorites sit above the list because those are the questions Miami drivers ask: shade, power and the garage they already trust.

05

Receipts that add up

Check out and the session lands in Activity, then in Statistics by day, week or month, as a chart or a table, so the totals are never a surprise.

06

A demo that says what it is

Setup screens name their sample data: a demo plate, a demo card, a demo pass. The prototype stays honest and nothing you type is saved.

Design system

Night purple, sunset pink, ocean cyan.

The Figma file was a calm teal system. For Miami it became Vice City: Ocean Drive neon, a striped sunset, palm silhouettes. Pink to orange marks every action, cyan marks the route and the live session, and yellow marks you on the map.

Type is Outfit for the interface with a Yellowtail script for the sign. Targets are 42 pixels or larger, numbers use tabular figures, and the app works with a keyboard and respects reduced motion.

My role

Concept, UX and visual design. Built with Claude.

I defined the flow, drew the wireframes and high-fidelity screens in Figma, and directed the Miami adaptation and the Vice City restyle. The prototype was written with Claude as a single HTML file with no framework. Photos are from Unsplash.

For a real launch the open work is the live part: garage inventory and bay availability from operators, payment authorization and receipts, plate recognition at the gate, turn-by-turn navigation and a plan for overstays.
